# Game Design — Working Brief

## High concept

An original first-person horror exploration game set in a neglected indoor play centre. The player explores a once-colourful children's venue whose play structures, service corridors, and staff spaces have fallen into disrepair. Two robotic attendants with contrasting operating modes form the central character concept.

The project uses the ruined daycare atmosphere from *Five Nights at Freddy's: Security Breach – RUIN* as a broad mood reference only. The game must establish its own identity.

## Experience goals

- Make the location feel like a real indoor play venue with a clear history and layout.
- Contrast cheerful design language with abandonment, silence, and deterioration.
- Encourage careful exploration and environmental storytelling.
- Make the two attendants feel distinct through original silhouettes, animation, sound, and behaviour.
- Build a small, polished playable slice before expanding the facility.

## Design pillars

1. **A believable place:** play structures, reception, party rooms, staff-only spaces, storage, and maintenance routes should connect logically.
2. **Unease over constant action:** use lighting, distant sounds, sightlines, and environmental clues to build tension.
3. **Readable character behaviour:** the attendants should have understandable rules and recognizable movement, not arbitrary teleporting or unavoidable pursuit.
4. **Original identity:** all final character designs, names, lore, level layouts, dialogue, and audio are original or properly licensed.

## First playable slice

The first milestone should be one compact section of the facility containing:
- A clear starting point and a visible exploration goal.
- A small loop of connected play and staff areas.
- Basic first-person movement and collision.
- A few interactable objects or environmental clues.
- One simple attendant encounter using placeholder art.
- A restart path and a clear end condition.

## Non-goals for the first slice

- A full-sized facility.
- Multiplayer.
- A finished story or voice cast.
- Final character models.
- Complex combat systems.
- Copying recognizable FNAF characters, locations, story beats, or assets.

## Technical direction

The current foundation is Three.js + Vite with JavaScript modules. Keep the starter kit operational while adding game systems in focused modules. Physics and AI libraries are candidates, not installed dependencies: evaluate them only when the prototype needs them.

## Open creative decisions

The final game title, facility name, attendant names and designs, story explanation, and exact player objective remain undecided.
